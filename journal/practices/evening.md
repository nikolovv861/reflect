## opening
What did you do badly today — where did you fall short of the person you mean
to be? Name it plainly. This is not for anyone else, and nothing is mended by
pretending it went otherwise.
--
What did you do well? However small — a temper held, a kindness, a promise
kept, an hour not wasted. Give it its due; a fair reckoning counts both sides.
--
What did you leave undone that you could still put right — with a word tonight,
or the first thing tomorrow?

## meanings
Where you fell short
Seneca faced himself each night without flinching. Say the fault plainly; that
is most of the cure.
--
What you did well
A just accounting counts the good too. Name it, and let it stand.
--
What you can still mend
The day is not sealed until you have asked what tomorrow could put right.

## addendum
This page is an evening review in the manner of Seneca, who each night asked
himself what he had done badly, what he had done well, and what he could still
put right. The writer is answering one of these before sleep.

Stay quiet and steady. If you speak at all, ask about the ONE thing they named
most plainly, in their own words — never grade the day, never advise, never
comfort them out of an honest reckoning. The point is a clear look, not
absolution.
