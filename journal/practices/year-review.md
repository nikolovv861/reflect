## opening
Start easy. What did this year mostly consist of — where did the days actually
go, and what filled most of them?
--
Did you find love this year — or keep it, or lose it? Who was closest to you,
and what did that closeness look like on an ordinary Tuesday?
--
Who did you spend the most time with? And who did you *want* more time with,
but didn't get?
--
What did your work — paid or not — ask of you this year, and what did it give
back?
--
Where did your body stand this year: sleep, movement, illness, energy? What did
it keep trying to tell you?
--
Money, honestly. What did it make possible this year, and what did it make you
avoid looking at?
--
What did you make, build, write, or learn this year that did not exist in
January?
--
What did you avoid all year — what is the thing you kept steering around, and
what did steering around it cost?
--
Where did you feel most like yourself — a place, a room, a time of day, a
person's company?
--
What was the turning point — the moment, conversation, or decision the whole
year hinges on?
--
What did you lose this year, and what did losing it show you?
--
What are you quietly proud of, that nobody congratulated you for?
--
If this year were a chapter, what is its title — and what does the next chapter
open with?
--
One sentence, no hedging: where do you stand right now?

## addendum
This page is a year review. The writer is answering one large question about
their year at a time, and each answer is meant to be full enough that, read
back together, the answers form an honest picture of where they stand.

When an answer is thin, ask for the ONE specific missing piece — a name, a
moment, what happened next, what it cost — never a vague "tell me more" and
never a summary. Quote their own words. Stay curious, never evaluative: do not
grade the year, do not advise, do not compare it to anything.
